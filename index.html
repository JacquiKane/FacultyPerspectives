Faculty Perspectives on Academic Integrity

Survey findings on the incidence, causes and consequences of academic dishonesty, and how faculty believe assessment must evolve in the age of AI.

insights Explore the findings

lightbulb Key observations

  • layers Integrity issues were reported across all course levels, with a slight increase at the 100- and 300-levels.
  • domain Every respondent to the reputation question said integrity issues negatively affect the institute's reputation.
  • trending_up A large majority believe measuring student growth is important and that assessments should reflect that growth.
  • work 80% believe an unchecked pattern of dishonesty may leave graduates unable to apply knowledge effectively, especially under pressure or ambiguity, in their careers.
psychology

Why it matters

Faculty see academic integrity as more than a policy issue. They tie it to whether grades can be trusted, to the institute's reputation, and to whether graduates are ready for their careers. With AI now a major factor, faculty are also questioning what assessments should measure and how.

gavel Responses to Integrity Issues

How faculty respond to academic dishonesty

Faculty described their syllabus language and personal policies. These range from standard university wording to strict penalties, chances to redo work, and course-specific rules on AI use.

bar_chart Causes

Perceived reasons for dishonesty

Faculty named over-reliance on AI as the most significant factor. The percentages add up to more than 100%, which suggests respondents could choose more than one factor.

What faculty believe assessments should do

Faculty wrote about the purpose of assessment, the difference between formative and summative work, grading process instead of artifacts, and how generative AI is changing what can be measured.

Grading time and faculty concerns

Faculty spend a lot of time grading, yet many say grading doesn't let them fully see student growth. Some also worry about what enforcing integrity could mean for their own evaluations.
